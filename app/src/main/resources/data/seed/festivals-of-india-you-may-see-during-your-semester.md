India has a festival for almost every season, and in Chennai you are likely to be invited into at
least one of them. Friends, hostel neighbours and staff are usually happy to explain what is going
on, and many will invite you to join. Say yes.

The dates below are from the Government of Tamil Nadu's list of public holidays for 2026. Most
festivals follow the lunar calendar, so they move from year to year. For how holidays affect
classes, see [[Academic Calendar 2026]].

## Navaratri and Golu — October

**Navaratri** means "nine nights", and in Tamil Nadu it is the season of **Golu**: families arrange
dolls and figurines of gods, animals and village scenes on an odd number of decorated steps. In the
evenings, neighbours visit each other's Golu, sing, and leave with a small gift, often a snack made
of chickpeas called sundal. If someone invites you to see their Golu, it is a real honour.

The nine nights end with **Ayudha Pooja** on **19 October 2026**, when people honour the tools of
their work (you may see scooters and laptops decorated with flowers), and **Vijayadasami** on
**20 October 2026**, considered a lucky day to begin learning something new.

## Deepavali — 8 November 2026

**Deepavali** (Diwali in the north) is the festival of lights. In Tamil Nadu the day begins before
dawn with an oil bath and new clothes, and continues with sweets shared between families, small
clay oil lamps (diyas) in every doorway, and fireworks from early morning until late at night.
Sweet shops are at their best in the week before, and boxes of sweets are the usual gift.

In 2026 Deepavali falls on **Sunday 8 November**.

## Pongal — mid-January

**Pongal** is Tamil Nadu's harvest festival and the most important one of the Tamil year. Over
several days, families cook new rice with milk and jaggery in a clay pot until it boils over, a sign
of abundance, and shout "Pongalo Pongal!" as it does. Doorsteps get their largest and most
colourful kolams of the year, and cattle are washed and decorated with painted horns.

In 2026 the holidays were **Pongal on 15 January**, **Thiruvalluvar Day on 16 January** and
**Uzhavar Thirunal** (Farmers' Day) **on 17 January**. If you arrive for the January semester, it
may be one of your first experiences of Tamil Nadu.

![A large, colourful kolam drawn for a festival in Chennai](/img/articles/festivals/pongal-kolam.jpg)
*At Pongal, kolams at the doorstep grow large and full of colour.*

## Holi — March

**Holi**, the spring festival of colours, is celebrated most widely in North India. People throw
coloured powder and water at each other, and by midday everyone looks the same. In Chennai it is
smaller, but campuses with students from all over India usually celebrate it with great energy.
Wear old clothes, and rub a little oil into your hair and skin beforehand so the colour washes
out more easily.

![Coloured powder left on the ground after Holi](/img/articles/festivals/holi-colours.jpg)

## Many more

Chennai also celebrates **Christmas** (25 December), **Ramzan** and **Bakrid**, **Vinayagar
Chathurthi** (14 September in 2026, with clay Ganesha idols in every street) and the **Tamil New
Year** in April. The city marks all of them, and on campus you will find friends to share each one
with.

For the customs that help at a family celebration, see
