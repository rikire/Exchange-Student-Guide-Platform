India is a country of more than a billion people, dozens of major languages and many religions, so
almost any rule about "Indian customs" has exceptions. What follows is what you are likely to meet
in Chennai and on campus, and it is enough to feel at home quickly.

## Saying hello

- **Vanakkam** (va-NUK-kum) is the Tamil greeting, said with palms pressed together in front of the
  chest. It works for hello and, with a smile, for goodbye.
- **Namaste** is the Hindi equivalent and is understood everywhere.
- **Nandri** (NUN-dree) means thank you in Tamil. Say it at the canteen and watch the reaction.
- A handshake is normal on campus. With someone you have just met, especially an older person, a
  vanakkam is a safe and warm choice.

You will also meet the famous head tilt, a gentle side-to-side movement of the head. Most of the
time it means "yes", "okay" or "I'm listening". You will probably be doing it yourself within a
month.

## Eating on a banana leaf

A traditional South Indian meal is served on a banana leaf: rice in the middle, with sambar (a
lentil and vegetable stew), rasam (a thin, peppery soup), vegetables, curd, pickle and a crisp
papadam around it. Waiters come round with refills, and you are welcome to have more.

![A South Indian meal served on a banana leaf](/img/articles/india/banana-leaf-meal.jpg)
*A full meal on a banana leaf: rice, curries, sweets and papadam.*

- **Use your right hand** to eat. Mix a little rice with sambar or rasam and bring it to your mouth
  with your fingertips. Spoons are always available if you ask.
- **Folding the leaf** when you finish: folding the top half towards you is commonly taken as saying
  the meal was good.
- **Vegetarian food** is everywhere and excellent. Many restaurants are entirely vegetarian and
  say "veg" on the sign.

## Coffee, the South Indian way

Chennai is a coffee city. **Filter coffee** (kaapi) is brewed slowly in a two-part steel filter,
mixed with hot milk and sugar, and poured from a height between a tumbler and a small bowl (the
dabara) until it froths. It costs little and tastes wonderful.

![A steel South Indian coffee filter](/img/articles/india/filter-coffee.jpg)
*The two-part steel filter that makes South Indian coffee.*

## Kolam at the doorstep

Early in the morning, many homes in Tamil Nadu have a **kolam** drawn at the entrance: a pattern of
dots and lines in white rice flour, sometimes filled with colour. It is a welcome sign and a daily
art form, and at festivals the kolams become large and brilliantly coloured.

![A colourful kolam drawn on the ground in Chennai](/img/articles/india/kolam.jpg)
*A festival kolam in Chennai.*

## Small customs that help

- Take your shoes off when you enter a home or a temple. A pile of shoes at the door is your hint.
- Pass and receive things, especially money and food, with your right hand.
- Dress a little more covered than you would at home when you visit temples or families: shoulders
  and knees covered is a good default.
- People may ask where you are from, whether you are married, or what your parents do. It is
  friendly curiosity, and a question back is the right answer.
