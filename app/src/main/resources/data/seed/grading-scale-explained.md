IIT Madras uses a letter-grade system, not a simple percentage or a US-style A–F scale. Here's the
full table, exactly as OGE lists it:

| Grade | Points |
|---|---|
| S | 10 |
| A | 9 |
| B | 8 |
| C | 7 |
| D | 6 |
| E | 4 |
| U | 0 |

## Which grades mean you passed

**Grades S through E all indicate successful completion of the course.** Only **U** (0 points) means
you did not pass. This is a wider "you're fine" band than many grading systems abroad use — a D or an
E here is still a completed, credited course, not a fail — but check with your home institution how
this scale converts back into *their* system before you assume a specific grade transfers cleanly.

## What this doesn't cover

This scale is separate from [[Attendance Requirements]], which is graded on its own percentage scale
and can affect your standing in a course independently of how well you actually performed
academically. A strong grade on the S-to-E scale does not protect you from an attendance problem, and
vice versa.

- How this scale is expected to convert into ECTS or other home-institution credit/grade systems for
  exchange-student transcripts specifically.
