Much of IIT Madras is protected forest, carved out of the neighbouring **Guindy National Park**, and
the animals did not leave when the institute arrived. You will meet some of them on your first day.
What follows is from the institute's [campus life page](https://www.iitm.ac.in/campus-life/overview),
the pages of **Prakriti**, the campus wildlife club
([about](https://home.iitm.ac.in/prakriti/prakriti), [wildlife helpline](https://home.iitm.ac.in/prakriti/prakriti/helpline.html)),
and student and press reports linked below.

## What lives here

The institute counts about **100 species of birds** and about **300 species of trees and plants** on
campus. The animals you are most likely to notice:

- **Spotted deer (chital):** the deer with white spots, in large numbers, often in herds along the
  roads and around the hostels. According to a nature-walk report they are not native here; they
  were introduced in 1940 ([casualwalker.com](https://casualwalker.com/?p=15874)).
- **Blackbuck:** an antelope, rarer than the deer and shyer. Males are dark with long spiral horns;
  females and young are light brown. The institute calls it "one of the most elegant antelopes in
  the world". It is a protected species in India.
- **Bonnet macaques:** the monkeys, named for the cap of hair on their heads. They live around the
  hostels and are the animal you will have the most to do with. See below.
- **Jackals, mongooses, palm civets ("toddy cats"), squirrels and wild cats:** listed by Prakriti.
  Jackals and civets are active at night and rarely seen.
- **Indian star tortoises:** small land tortoises with star patterns on the shell.
- **Snakes and other reptiles:** they stay in bushes and forest patches, but cross roads and muddy
  paths at night. Prakriti lists the rat snake as common and the harmless green vine snake; it names
  the **cobra** among the snakes found here.
- **Butterflies:** about 40 species, according to Prakriti.
- **Stray dogs and cats:** not wildlife, but present. Dog packs on campus attack deer, and they can
  chase people too.

## Monkeys: keep your food and your room closed

Bonnet macaques have learned that hostels mean food. Students report them going through corridor
dustbins, coming into rooms through open doors and windows and leaving with food and belongings
([The Fifth Estate](https://www.t5eiitm.org/insti-wildlife-for-dummies/),
[PaGaLGuY](https://www.pagalguy.com/articles/monkey-business-at-iit-madras-6272?amp=1)).

- **Close your window and lock your door** when you leave the room, even for a few minutes. A
  window left open is how most raids start.
- **Do not leave food out**, including in a bag on your balcony or desk near the window.
- **Do not feed them**, and do not eat in front of them outdoors. A monkey that has been fed once
  comes back and expects more.
- **Carry food in a closed bag**, not a plastic carrier they can see into and grab.
- **If one comes close, do not stare at it, and do not threaten it with a stick or rod.** Prakriti's
  advice is that they generally do not attack; walk past calmly without looking at them. Staring
  and raising a stick read as a challenge.
- **If a monkey takes something, let it go.** Chasing it is how people get scratched.

## Deer and blackbuck: look, do not feed

The deer are calm and will let you walk quite close. That does not make them pets.

- **Do not feed them**, especially not packaged food. Deer on campus have died from eating plastic
  ([The News Minute, 2016](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/10-days-2-deer-iit-madras-have-been-found-dead-due-plastic-waste-53557)).
  Put waste in a closed bin, and never leave plastic bags in the open.
- **Keep your distance from males with antlers**, especially when two are fighting. Prakriti asks
  people not to interfere: fighting within a species is normal territorial behaviour. A deer
  bleeding a little where it has shed its antlers is also normal.
- **Slow down on a bicycle or e-rickshaw.** Deer step onto the road without looking, mostly at dawn
  and dusk.
- **Do not chase them for a photo.** The blackbuck especially is easily stressed.

## Snakes

You are unlikely to see one in the daytime, but be careful at night.

- **Carry a torch, or use your phone's, on dark paths at night**, and stay on the road rather than
  cutting through grass.
- **If you see a snake, do not try to kill it or catch it.** Move away, watch where it goes from a
  distance, and call Security (below). Security has staff trained to remove snakes from buildings.
- **If someone is bitten,** keep them still and calm and get them to the **Institute Hospital**
  straight away: call the ambulance on **044 2257 8888**. Do not cut the wound, suck it, or tie a
  tight band around the limb. Try to remember what the snake looked like; do not go after it to
  photograph it.

## Bites and scratches from monkeys or dogs

Monkeys and dogs can carry **rabies**, which is fatal once symptoms start and preventable before.
Any bite, or a scratch that breaks the skin:

1. Wash the wound with soap and running water for at least 15 minutes.
2. Go to the **Institute Hospital** the same day, even if the wound looks small. You may need a
   course of anti-rabies vaccine. See [[Health Care and Wellness Support]].
3. Tell your hostel warden or OGE if you will miss classes for the follow-up doses.

## Tortoises and injured animals

- **Star tortoises live on land.** Do not put one in water. Keeping one is illegal. If one is
  crossing a road, Prakriti suggests picking it up slowly and placing it in the undergrowth on the
  side it was heading to.
- **An injured animal** (a deer hit by a vehicle, for example): do not move it, do not crowd it, and
  keep other people back. Call Security and tell them where it is. Prakriti warns not to put an
  injured deer on a smooth cement floor, where it can slip and break a leg.
- **Someone hurting an animal** (throwing stones, a catapult, traps): report it to Security.
