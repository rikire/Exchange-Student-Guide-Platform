If you make one trip out of Chennai during your semester, make it this one. **Mahabalipuram**
(also called Mamallapuram) is a small town by the sea, about 60 km south of the city along the East
Coast Road, and it holds some of the most beautiful stone carving in India. The **Group of Monuments
at Mahabalipuram**, built by the Pallava kings in the 7th and 8th centuries, has been a UNESCO World
Heritage Site since 1984.

The town is compact: the main monuments are within walking distance of each other, and the sea is
always a few minutes away.

## The Shore Temple

The **Shore Temple** stands right at the edge of the Bay of Bengal, as it has for about 1,300
years, with the waves just behind it. It is one of the oldest structural stone temples in South
India, built of granite blocks rather than carved out of a single rock. Come in the late afternoon,
when the stone turns golden.

![The Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram by the sea](/img/articles/mahabalipuram/shore-temple.jpg)
*The Shore Temple, beside the Bay of Bengal.*

## The Pancha Rathas

The **Pancha Rathas** ("five chariots") are five temples, each carved from a single piece of rock,
together with a life-size stone elephant, lion and bull. Each is in a different style, as if the
sculptors were trying out ideas, and they are named after the five Pandava brothers of the
Mahabharata.

![The Pancha Rathas, five monolithic temples at Mahabalipuram](/img/articles/mahabalipuram/pancha-rathas.jpg)
*The Pancha Rathas, each carved from a single rock.*

## Arjuna's Penance

**Arjuna's Penance**, also called the **Descent of the Ganges**, is one of the largest open-air
rock reliefs in the world: a whole cliff face carved with gods, people, and animals, including a
family of life-size elephants. Take your time with it: every visit reveals a new detail, such as a
cat standing on one leg in imitation of a holy man.

![The carved rock relief of Arjuna's Penance with its elephants](/img/articles/mahabalipuram/arjunas-penance.jpg)
*Arjuna's Penance, carved into a single rock face.*

Just up the hill is **Krishna's Butter Ball**, a huge round boulder that seems to balance on a
slope, and a favourite spot for photographs.

## Planning the day

- **Getting there:** by cab along the East Coast Road, which follows the coast and passes the
  beaches of [[Chennai's Beaches]]. Sharing a cab with friends makes it easy and inexpensive.
- **Timing:** start in the morning, see the Pancha Rathas and Arjuna's Penance before the midday
  heat, have lunch in town, and finish at the Shore Temple and the beach in the late afternoon.
- **Tickets:** the Shore Temple and the Pancha Rathas need a ticket from the Archaeological Survey
  of India, which can be bought online; the fee for foreign nationals is different from the one for
  Indian citizens.
- **Stone carvers:** the town still has working sculptors, and you can watch them in their
  workshops along the main streets.
