Welcome to Chennai. It is the capital of the state of Tamil Nadu, it sits on the Bay of Bengal, and
until 1996 its official name was Madras. You will still hear both names, often in the same
sentence, and the university you are studying at still carries the old one.

The first language here is **Tamil**, one of the oldest living languages in the world. English is
widely used on campus, in shops and on signs, so you can manage from day one. A few words of Tamil
will still open doors, and people are visibly delighted when you try: see
[[India in Your First Weeks: Greetings, Food and Everyday Customs]].

## A city of old buildings in bright colours

Chennai grew around the East India Company's Fort St George, and many of its best-known buildings
stand close to each other in the old centre, so one afternoon covers several:

- **Chennai Central station**, the red-brick building with the clock tower, is where trains leave
  for the rest of India. It is worth seeing even if you are not catching a train.
- **Ripon Building**, completed in 1913, is the white neoclassical home of the city corporation,
  right next to Central.
- **Fort St George**, where the city began, now houses the state legislature and a museum.

![The white Ripon Building with its clock tower](/img/articles/chennai/ripon-building.jpg)
*Ripon Building, the seat of the Greater Chennai Corporation, beside Chennai Central.*

## Mylapore: temples, tanks and coffee

If you visit one neighbourhood in your first weeks, make it **Mylapore**. The **Kapaleeshwarar
Temple**, dedicated to Shiva, rises above its large temple tank, and its tower (gopuram) is covered
in hundreds of painted figures. Around it are flower sellers, small shops selling brass lamps and
cotton saris, and some of the best places in the city for **filter coffee**, served frothy in a
steel tumbler and saucer.

Visitors of every faith are welcome at most temples. Take your shoes off at the entrance (there is
usually a counter that keeps them), dress with shoulders and knees covered, and ask before taking
photographs inside.

![Kapaleeshwarar Temple's tower rising behind its temple tank](/img/articles/chennai/kapaleeshwarar-temple-tank.jpg)
*The Kapaleeshwarar Temple and its tank in Mylapore.*

## The music season

From late November to mid-January, Chennai holds one of the largest classical music and dance
festivals in the world, known as the **Margazhi** or December season. Hundreds of concert halls
(sabhas) host Carnatic music and Bharatanatyam dance, many performances are free or cheap, and the
canteens that open for the season are famous in their own right. If your stay reaches December, it
is one of the most memorable things you can do here.
